**Fluidyne Cavity Systems : Dancing Pressure Lines**

--

Hydraulic amplification was among the earliest of the problems solved by the Siayeo - after the gear, lever, and pulley, the valve is considered in the Nexus as one of the fundamental classes of simple machines. Yet in practice, the common proportional valve requires equal precision from both control and driver signals, the maintenance of which is a struggle endemic to fluidyne machinery; while mechanical couplings are inherently suited to precision via physical indexing, precise proportional valve control cannot in practice be achieved by mundane electro-mechanical, fluidyne, or even hydro-mechanical couplings, which are subject to drift, transmission losses, and dynamic disturbances, all of which place a heavy burden on strictly proportional systems.

In 957 AC, the retired Yiwa acolyte and former royal advisor Horiang Taga compiled and published a cavity-based system of flow deflectors and integrators inspired by the gently tumbling whorls of the Great Tree's memory pools: by merging and splitting flows in chambers that amplify transient vortical resonances, the system gained the ability to encode, decode, and synchronize discrete hydrodynamic cascades in a self-regulating process. Elements of this ingenious process would contribute to the fluidyne routing and processing technologies pioneered in Shisha, but the original use case - the discretization of flows into expressive fluidyne 'words' - would largely go ignored throughout the 10th century due to its prohibitive complexity, falling into almost complete obscurity during the 981-989 prohibition levied upon mechanical temple interpreters by the Branching Divine (link: Siayeo Cybernetics).

The Millennium Steel initiatives of the 990s would rediscover Horiang's treatises on cavity discretization in a series of inquiries by Tsninah Temple (in Urwe) into the construction of a number of highly accurate fluidyne clocks - evacuated from temples in Loa in 991 amidst the worsening civil war, it was noted that unlike other pure fluidyne timekeepers, the devices had not suffered any issues from fluctuating atmospheric conditions during their time at sea, indeed approaching the performance of the onboard mechanical clocks. In a departure from the original treatise, however, the strengths of the cavity system would be recognized not for their inherent fidelity, but for their ability to translate and amplify extremely rough inputs - by 993, they would be pressed into the role of transducing pings from acoustic slag probes in the furnaces and foundries of Millennium Steel associate firms.

Buoyed by the vast scope and widespread success of the Millennium Steel initiatives, fluidyne cavity systems quickly became the backbone of numerous acoustic systems, including the first Siayeo marine echolocation system in 995. The deployment of sonar by Shisha survey vessels would prove crucial to the success of the laying of the Yunamer Transoceanic Essence Conduit in 996, and meteorological acoustic probes would go on to help bring about the monumental achievement of widespread and reliable 3-day weather predictions by the year 1000 in the states of Angga, Yiwa, Shisha, Urwe, and Eiyanixie. In recognition of his contributions, the 1001 Toumay Science and Technology Convention would posthumously award Horiang Taga a sum of 10,000 Doyan and honorary recognition as a member of the Yiwa Scientific Advisory Committee.

Despite the celebrated signal processing capabilities of the cavity system, its complexity and intolerance to manufacturing defects would limit its use in actuating mechanisms until the year 1014, when the Yegorum Technical Institute in Yineng succeeded in constructing a compact electrohydraulic servomotor system using cavity acoustic probes and a binary electromechanical valve. While this would go on to become an invaluable component in Siayeo industry, as showcased by SWIFAC in Shisha, its rapid adoption is mainly attributable to the Yegorum Technical Institute's extensive participation in military research: cavity fluidyne servos quickly found their way into rangefinders, aircraft gun-laying systems, powered loading systems, and in 1022, the first military walker. Engineers were quick to apply the expertise acquired from these state-funded programs to business pursuits subsidized by cheap Velkotran manufacturing, leading to the widespread mechanization of Siayeo fabrication in sync with the dawning Velkotran industrial juggernaut of 11th century.
