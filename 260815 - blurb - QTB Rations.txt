**Blurbs : QTB Depot Rations**

--

Even in the distant embassies and military bases of the QTB, a taste of home is never far - but certainly at an arm's length, being subject to the demanding ration specifications of an administration that must deal with the ever-fickle procurement schedules of committees occasionally hamstrung by political vetos. Though it has presently become a matter of prestige for each member state to contribute to the culinary options of QTB diplomats and soldiers, real or political ignorance of nutritional realities and decades of neglect during the Great Peace have given good cause for the QTB's logistical services to take their own, unique approaches to ensuring a nutritious and agreeable diet for their diverse constituents.

QTB Depot Service rations typically use a diverse admixture of cheap ingredients within each nutrition group, a decision made both to offset their monotony and reduce vulnerability to supply chain disruptions. Starch alternates between ubiquitous yams, imported pantherclaw, central Qayan breadnuts, and Yeoxli millet; vegetables, though overwhelmingly represented by kelp and pantherclaw greens, are supplemented by preserves of regionally cultivated herbs. Protein is mainly seafood-based, but occasionally is represented by ground meat from chickens and bush rats. Spice mixes vary across deployments and procurement settings, but span a diverse set of spicy, smoky, peppery, acrid, bitter, tangy, woody, fruity, and numbing flavours. Notably, salt packets are not included - what little salt intake is needed by ymosuri is often fulfilled thrice over by the amount used for preservation of foodstuffs.

While diplomatic workers typically have periodic access to local restaurants and metropolitan cuisines, most QTB staff and soldiers end up in various remote outposts, islands, and floating platforms maintained by QTB initiatives. Those formerly in employ of the QTB often recall finding comfort in the ostensibly mediocre, but remarkably palatable QTB Depot Service rations - particularly those created in the aftermath of the 1026 Medijan Crisis, which gave rise to a confluence of massive QTB funding and an influx of foodstuffs secured by the economic support of the newly established Middle Sea Pact. As a result, the food culture of the QTB Depot Service has spread to canteens and soup kitchens across the Siayeo Nexus.

Examples :

Depot Ration 1028A
- 3 x 50(200)g freeze dried yam cakes
- 1 x 50g canned pepper shrimp mash with  sprouts
- 2 x 5g cinnamon-cocoa-butter brick
- 8 x 1g dried pantherclaw leaves
- 4 x 0.45g pressed seaweed wafers
- 1 x 30(300mL)g nut milk+flour dry brick
- 0.45g spice mix, assorted
- 5g sugar
Consumable dry, hydrated with boiling water to make curry, wraps, and a sweet drink, or alternatively a slower meal of green mash on dense flatbreads with salty salad.

Depot Ration 1028B
- 4 x 50g pantherclaw-millet biscuits
- 1 x 50(200)g freeze dried yam cake
- 2 x 10(30)g freeze dried smoked fish fillet
- 8 x 1g dried pantherclaw leaves
- 1 x 20g salted strangler bud butter
- 1 x 20(100)g whitehair fern mash brick
- 1 x 10(100mL)g candy coated coffee pellets (long-life water-resistant instant coffee)
- 0.45g spice mix, assorted
Consumable dry, can be steamed or hydrated in boiling water to make a platter with coffee, a thick green stew, or wrapped and fried peppery fish cakes.

Each ingredient is either independently spoilage-resistant, canned, or covered in a lacquer-sealed paper wrapping.
